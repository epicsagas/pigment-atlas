# Refik Anadol: In-Depth Research Report

[한국어](../01-research-report.md) | **English**

> Research date: 2026-10-15. "Refik Anotol" in the original request was interpreted as the Turkish American media artist **Refik Anadol**. Figures and dates follow the sources in `sources.md`; where sources disagree, both values are given in the text.

## 1. Summary

Refik Anadol (born November 7, 1985, in Istanbul) is a media artist who works with "data as pigment and machine intelligence as collaborator." In 2014 he founded Refik Anadol Studio (RAS) in Los Angeles with Efsun Erkılıç, and on June 20, 2026, he opened DATALAND, which he calls "the world's first museum of AI arts," at The Grand LA in downtown Los Angeles.

His work falls into three broad periods.

1. **Data painting on architecture (2008 to 2015)**: projection mapping turns building surfaces and whole rooms into moving canvases. Key works: *Infinity Room* and *Visions of America: Amériques*.
2. **Machine hallucinations (2016 to 2023)**: after the Google AMI residency, he visualizes the latent space of huge archives with GANs and dimensionality reduction (t-SNE, UMAP). This period includes *Archive Dreaming*, *WDCH Dreams*, the *Machine Hallucinations* series, *Unsupervised* at MoMA, and the exterior of the Las Vegas Sphere.
3. **In-house models based on nature and architecture (2024 to present)**: the Large Nature Model (LNM), trained on permission-based nature data; the Large Architecture Model (LAM), fine-tuned on architectural archives; and DATALAND, which makes them permanent.

Opinion is sharply divided. MoMA added *Unsupervised* to its collection (its first generative artwork), and visitors stayed an average of 38 minutes. The critic Jerry Saltz, by contrast, dismissed the same work as "a massive techno lava lamp" and "a half-million-dollar screensaver." This tension is itself central to understanding Anadol's work.

### Intellectual starting points

- **Lev Manovich's "augmented space"**: as an undergraduate he read this paper and was struck by its claim that collaboration between architects and artists could make "the invisible flow of data visible." He began using the term "data painting" with his first outdoor projection in 2008, and this became his working principle of using "light as material and data as pigment."
- **Blade Runner and memory**: the motif of memories implanted in a machine mind later leads to the core question of Machine Hallucinations: if a machine can learn, it can also remember, dream, and hallucinate.
- **DeepDream (2016)**: Google's release of the DeepDream algorithm during his first Google AMI residency was the direct trigger for that question.

## 3. Major works by period

### 3.1 Data painting on architecture (2008 to 2015)

- **Quadrature (2010)**: a live A/V performance with fellow student Alican Aktürk in the main building of santralistanbul. Footage of quadrilaterals was manipulated on the building to explore the relationship between architecture and media. He followed it with *Quadrangle* at the SANAA building in Essen, Germany.
- **Augmented Structures v1.0 (2011, Istanbul Biennial)**: sounds recorded at different times of day on İstiklal Avenue were reinterpreted and projected with nine projectors.
- **Infinity Room (developed at UCLA in 2012, shown at Zorlu PSM for the 2015 Istanbul Biennial)**: a "temporary immersive environment experiment" that uses pixels and 3D projection mapping instead of mirror illusions to turn every surface of a room into an abstract infinite space. It toured to SXSW, the New Zealand Festival, ZKM, and elsewhere.
- **Visions of America: Amériques (2014)**: at the 2013 Microsoft Research Design Expo he proposed using the exterior walls of Walt Disney Concert Hall (WDCH) as a canvas. With the original 3D model from Gehry Technologies, he created an indoor architectural video installation for the LA Phil's performance of Varèse's *Amériques* (his UCLA thesis project).

### 3.2 Machine hallucinations (2016 to 2023)

- **Wind of Boston: Data Paintings (2017)**: a year of wind data from Boston Logan Airport (speed, direction, gusts, time, and temperature at 20-second intervals) visualized as four "chapters" on a 6 × 13 foot digital canvas. A model early "data painting" that turns the invisible flow of wind into fluid brushstrokes.
- **Archive Dreaming (2017, SALT Galata)**: 1.7 million documents from SALT Research sorted with machine learning. A t-SNE point cloud spreads the whole archive through 3D space, and a DCGAN generates "alternative documents that never existed." Visitors explore the archive inside a responsive environment.
- **Melting Memories (2018, Pilevneli Gallery, February 7 to March 17)**: prompted by his uncle's Alzheimer's diagnosis, he worked with UCSF's Neuroscape lab. Anonymous EEG data drives visualizations of memory, health, degeneration, and decay.
- **WDCH Dreams (September 2018)**: a commission for the LA Phil's centennial. Trained on 587,763 images, 1,880 videos, 1,483 metadata files, and 17,773 audio files (44.5 TB in total), it was projected across the entire exterior of WDCH with 42 projectors (50K resolution) and 8-channel sound. A 12-minute piece in three parts (*Centennial Memories*, *Consciousness*, *Dream*) ran every 30 minutes over ten nights. Gehry's 3D files let the studio map the building's curves exactly. Indoors, a companion touchscreen installation let visitors explore the archive non-linearly.
- **Latent History (2019, Fotografiska Stockholm)**: about 150 years of Stockholm's photographic archive reinterpreted with AI.
- **Machine Hallucinations: NYC (2019, Artechouse NYC)**: the first work in the series. Wikipedia describes 300 million photos of New York plus 113 million additional data points (subway sounds, radio, traffic noise); the artist's site says "over 100 million photographic memories." For privacy, only public images were used, and StyleGAN generated the "dreams."
- **Quantum Memories (2020, NGV Triennial)**: more than 200 million public nature-related images combined with quantum computing research data from the Google AI Quantum team. Shown on the largest LED screen in NGV's history, it won an iF Design Award in 2022.
- **Machine Hallucinations: Nature Dreams (2021, König Galerie Berlin)**: more than 300 million images of flowers, trees, mushrooms, landscapes, and clouds, then the largest raw nature dataset used for an artwork.
- **Casa Batlló residency (2021) and Living Architecture: Casa Batlló (May 2022)**: as Casa Batlló's first artist in residence he created the six-sided cube room *In the Mind of Gaudí*. The following year, a 360-degree façade work trained on real-time climate data from sensors around the building and about one billion images (Gaudí's sketches, the building's archives, and more) drew 65,000 people.
- **Unsupervised - Machine Hallucinations - MoMA (opened 2022-11-19, ran until 2023-10-29)**: analyzed in detail in section 4.
- **Sense of Healing (2022 onward)**: commissioned for the LuisaViaRoma × UNICEF gala. An AI data sculpture made with Therme Art and MYND from the brain data of people in mental health treatment.
- **Living Paintings (February 2023, Jeffrey Deitch LA)**: his first major US solo exhibition, using Pacific wind forecasts, real-time weather, and images of California's national parks.
- **Glacier Dreams (March 2023, Art Dubai)**: a work based on glacier data.
- **Machine Hallucinations: Sphere (September 2023 onward)**: the opening work for the Las Vegas Sphere, a 366-foot-tall building with a 580,000-square-foot LED shell. Part one used 1.1 million public images from the ISS, Hubble, and other sources; part two used 300 million national park images and wind sensor data from Las Vegas. NVIDIA A100s generated real-time AI animation, and it was then the largest AI artwork in the world. The artist called the premiere "one of the most Blade Runner moments ever."

### 3.3 In-house models based on nature and architecture (2024 to present)

- **Large Nature Model (LNM) and Living Archive: Nature (January 2024, WEF Davos)**: a generative AI model dedicated exclusively to nature, trained on images, sounds, and research from institutions such as the Natural History Museum in London and the Smithsonian. It is billed as "the world's first open-source generative AI model dedicated to nature."
- **Echoes of the Earth: Living Archive (2024-02-16 to 04-07, Serpentine North, London)**: the gallery walls were wrapped in imagery from data on flora, fauna, and fungi from more than 16 rainforest locations (including LiDAR and photogrammetry). More than 66,000 people visited in 47 days. It continued as his first solo exhibition in Asia at Futura Seoul in Gahoe-dong, Bukchon, Seoul (the exact opening date could not be confirmed in this research).
- **Large Nature Model: Living Art (March 2024, NVIDIA GTC, San Jose)** and **Large Nature Model: Coral (September 2024, UN Headquarters; Noor Riyadh 2024)**.
- **Winds of Yawanawá (2024)**: an NFT collection combining weather data from the Yawanawá village in the Amazon with artworks by young Yawanawá artists. It raised $3 million for the community.
- **AI Data Painting of the Keystone-Mast Collection (2024-09-21 to 2025-12-15, UCR California Museum of Photography)**: a reinterpretation of 250,000 stereoscopic glass-plate negatives and 100,000 prints.
- **Living Architecture: Gehry (2025-03-07 to 10-19, Guggenheim Museum Bilbao)**: the opening work of the Guggenheim's site-specific series *in situ*. The Large Architecture Model (LAM), an image diffusion model fine-tuned on Gehry's architectural language, generates an impossible building every minute, randomly varying location and time of day. Images are upscaled from a compact latent space to 16K, running continuously on Google Cloud. The work has six chapters: Data Universe, Data Plotting, Data Tunnel, Large Architecture Model, Machine Hallucination, and Dreams. Sound is by Kerim Karaoğlu.
- **Living Architecture: Biophilia (2025-05-10 to 11-23, Venice)**: installed during the 2025 Venice Architecture Biennale, where the artist also gave a keynote at the Biennale conference.
- **Living Memory: Messi, A Goal in Life (2025)**: Messi's header in the 2009 UEFA Champions League final turned into an AI data sculpture. Unveiled on June 11, 2025, it sold at Christie's for $1.87 million, with proceeds donated to educational initiatives in Latin America.
- **DATALAND and Machine Dreams: Rainforest (opened 2026-06-20, exhibition until 2027-01-31)**: covered in section 5.

## 4. Case study: *Unsupervised* (MoMA, 2022 to 2023)

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Source data | 138,151 collection records MoMA published on GitHub in 2016 (title, artist, medium, dimensions, date made, date acquired, classification) |
| Model | NVIDIA's StyleGAN2-ADA (adaptive discriminator augmentation), trained on subsets of the collection |
| Embeddings | 1024 dimensions, then clustered by theme to capture semantic context |
| Exploration tool | Latent Space Browser, custom software the studio has developed since 2017 |
| Visualization | Each data connection is drawn by an edge-detection algorithm and colored by the density of its previous and next latent coordinates, tracing the machine's "unconscious decisions" as lines |
| Fluid variation | *Fluid Dreams*: data pigment flows via a GPU-accelerated fluid solver and real-time ray-traced lighting |
| Data Universe variation | A "latent walk" built with UMAP over seven dimensions: x, y, z, r, g, b, and time. UMAP's inventor Leland McInnes praised it |
| Display | A roughly 24 × 24 foot media wall in MoMA's Gund Lobby, with trompe l'oeil depth at the screen edges |
| Reception | Extended four times after opening and ran for almost a year. Visitors stayed an average of 38 minutes. Acquired by MoMA in October 2023 |

The title (unsupervised learning) reflects the artist's point that "categorization requires a human perspective; without categories, the machine can find a new form." This reveals the core grammar of the work: **move the archive into a high-dimensional vector space, glide through that space like a camera, and render the movement as fluid and light.**

## 5. DATALAND (2026)

- **Location and scale**: The Grand LA (designed by Frank Gehry, 100 S Grand Ave), in the same cultural corridor as WDCH, The Broad, and MOCA. The public exhibition space is 25,000 square feet (Wikipedia gives 20,000 square feet). Gensler collaborated on the design, and Arup served as prime engineer and AV owner's representative.
- **Opening exhibition *Machine Dreams: Rainforest***: five galleries, including the Discovery Portal, the Data Pavilion with a 200-channel sound system, a gallery of cultural material shared with the permission of the Yawanawá community, and the Sanctuary, which visualizes aggregated audience interaction. It uses 1.5 billion pixels of high-resolution imagery, LiDAR and remote-sensing scans, more than 50,000 audio recordings, and real-time signals (humidity, light, soil moisture) from 16 rainforest locations.
- **Data.Link**: a wearable that pairs a wrist-worn Empatica device (spelled "Empathica" in the Artsy article) with a scent-diffusing neckband. The exhibition responds in real time to anonymized biometric and behavioral signals, and at the end each visitor receives a paper "data token" representing their visit, which they may keep or choose to delete.
- **Ethics and sustainability claims**: the museum says the model was trained on permission-based data from partners such as the Smithsonian, the Natural History Museum in London, Getty, and the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. It claims the LNM runs in a low-carbon Google Cloud region in Oregon on 87% carbon-free energy. The artist has said each visit uses roughly the energy of one iPhone charge. The press (Artsy) noted that these claims are likely to be closely scrutinized.
- **Additional platforms**: the open-access *Living Encyclopedia*, which gathers data on oceans, coral reefs, rainforests, and the atmosphere, and the DATALAND app for exchanging questions and answers with an AI inside the galleries.

## 6. Visual grammar: recurring vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Representative works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data Pigment | Translating data values into the physical qualities of color and particles | Wind of Boston, Living Paintings |
| Data Universe | Spreading an archive through 3D like a nebula via dimensionality reduction (t-SNE, UMAP) | Archive Dreaming, Unsupervised: Data Universe, Living Architecture: Gehry chapter 1 |
| Latent Walk | Continuous interpolation through the latent space of a GAN or diffusion model, so forms transform endlessly | Machine Hallucinations throughout |
| Fluid Dreams | Pigment flowing and swirling via a GPU fluid solver | Unsupervised Fluid Dreams, Sphere |
| Data Sculpture | A trompe l'oeil volume that seems to have depth inside its frame | Unsupervised, Coral Dreams, Messi |
| Living Architecture | Projection or LED skins fitted to a building's actual 3D form | WDCH Dreams, Casa Batlló, Sphere |
| Infinity Room | An immersive room in which every surface is a projection surface | Infinity Room, In the Mind of Gaudí |
| Data Tunnel | A first-person flight through latent space | Living Architecture: Gehry chapter 3 |

## 7. Criticism and debate

1. **The spectacle critique**: Jerry Saltz (New York/Vulture) called *Unsupervised* "a massive techno lava lamp," "a half-million-dollar screensaver," and a digital version of the "Zombie Formalism of the 2010s." His point is that it is a derivative that fails to transcend its source material and "takes away art's otherness." The two later had a public online spat (reported by Artnet).
2. **Art-historical precedent**: in the same piece Saltz cited the digital transformations of Jason Salavon and Jeremy Blake in the early 2000s, saying *Unsupervised* "adds nothing but scale and expense."
3. **The defense**: Feral File and others see *Unsupervised* as a "historical moment" where three early-2020s currents met: immersive installation, NFTs, and AI. The 38-minute dwell time and MoMA's acquisition are often cited as evidence of its public appeal.
4. **Data ethics and environmental cost**: early on he described using "publicly available images," but since 2024 he has foregrounded "permission-based" data and low-carbon computing. This can be read as a response to public demands about data provenance and carbon cost, and at DATALAND these claims have become subject to scrutiny.
5. **NFTs and the market**: since 2020 he has released more than a dozen collections and over 11,000 tokens, raising about $10 million for charity in total. This is entangled with criticism about the boundary between art and the market.

## 9. Implications for implementation

Anadol's work is a pipeline of **(1) large-scale data collection, (2) training and embedding, (3) latent space exploration, (4) real-time rendering, and (5) architectural display**. Of these, what a web browser and three.js can reproduce is the visual grammar of steps (3) to (5). Rather than reproducing the trained models themselves, it is realistic to implement the following as GPU shaders:

- Data Universe, spreading high-dimensional data into a 3D point cloud
- Data Pigment fluids, advecting particles with curl noise or real wind data
- Latent Walk morphs that interpolate endlessly between forms
- Data Sculpture and Infinity Room, with depth inside a frame

The concrete step-by-step plan is in `05-threejs-plan.md`. This project is a technique study and homage; it does not copy or use the artist's original videos, images, or datasets.
